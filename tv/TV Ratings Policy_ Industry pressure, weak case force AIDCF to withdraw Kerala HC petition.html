TV Ratings Policy: Industry pressure, weak case force AIDCF to withdraw Kerala HC petition

Senior television industry executives told e4m that the withdrawal was driven by a combination of legal setbacks and increasing industry pressure to restore certainty to the TV measurement ecosystem

by Imran Fazal
Published: Jul 29, 2026 9:57 AM  | 4 min read
Follow Us
  • The All India Digital Cable Federation (AIDCF) has withdrawn its writ petition against the Centre's Television Rating Policy, 2026, following increased pressure from broadcasters and advertisers for clarity in television audience measurement.
  • The petition challenged a provision that excludes viewership from television landing pages in official ratings, but the Kerala High Court vacated an interim stay, allowing the government to implement the policy.
  • Industry executives indicated that the withdrawal reflects a pragmatic decision to end uncertainty in the television measurement ecosystem, as the legal position had weakened and broader industry support was lacking.
  • The move clears the way for the implementation of revised ratings that aim to enhance the credibility of audience measurement by focusing on intentional viewing rather than passive exposure through landing pages.

The All India Digital Cable Federation (AIDCF) has withdrawn its writ petition before the Kerala High Court challenging the Centre's Television Rating Policy, 2026, a move that industry executives said reflects both mounting pressure from broadcasters and advertisers to end prolonged uncertainty around television audience measurement as well as the federation's weakening legal position after the court vacated its interim stay last week.

According to a Withdrawal Memo dated July 28, 2026, filed before the High Court of Kerala at Ernakulam, AIDCF sought permission to withdraw its writ petition while reserving the liberty to institute a fresh petition on the same or similar cause of action in the future.

The federation had challenged the proviso to Clause 5.4.1 of the Television Rating Policy, 2026, notified by the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB) on March 27. The provision excludes viewership generated through television landing pages from official television ratings.

The petition had sought to quash the clause "insofar as it excludes viewership arising out of Landing Pages from viewership measurement" and restrain authorities from implementing the operational guidelines issued under the policy.

The withdrawal memo, however, offers little explanation for the decision.

It merely states that the petitioners, "on account of certain subsequent developments and for reasons, do not propose to prosecute the above writ petition," while seeking liberty to approach the court again if required.

The development comes just days after the Kerala High Court vacated the interim stay that had prevented the Centre from implementing the landing page exclusion. In its July 24 order, the court held that the exclusion of landing page viewership could remain operational pending adjudication of the matter, effectively clearing the way for the government to enforce the revised ratings framework.

Industry sees legal strategy collapse

Several senior television industry executives told e4m that the withdrawal was driven by a combination of legal setbacks and increasing industry pressure to restore certainty to the television measurement ecosystem.

"The legal position became increasingly difficult after the interim stay was vacated. Once the court allowed the government to operationalize the policy pending final adjudication, the entire premise of seeking immediate judicial intervention weakened considerably," said a senior broadcast industry executive, requesting anonymity.
Another senior executive said the petition had failed to gain wider industry support because the government's rationale for excluding landing pages had become increasingly difficult to challenge.

"The arguments were simply not persuasive enough. Landing pages artificially inflated channel reach without reflecting genuine viewer choice. Defending that practice became increasingly difficult after both advertisers and broadcasters started pushing for cleaner and more credible audience measurement," the executive said.

A third industry executive described the withdrawal as a pragmatic decision rather than a legal victory.

"There was significant pressure across the ecosystem to end the uncertainty. Advertisers, broadcasters and agencies have all been waiting for stable ratings. Continuing the litigation after the stay was vacated would only have prolonged uncertainty without materially improving AIDCF's legal prospects."

Long-running dispute over landing pages

Landing pages have been one of the most contentious issues in India's television measurement system.

Under the practice, viewers switching on their television sets are automatically shown a particular channel before making an active viewing choice. Broadcasters and advertisers have argued for years that such automatically generated impressions distort audience measurement by recording passive exposure rather than intentional viewing.

The MIB's Television Rating Policy, 2026, sought to address the issue by directing that landing page viewership should not form part of official television ratings.

The provision represented one of the most significant reforms in India's television measurement framework in recent years and followed prolonged consultations between the ministry, the industry and the Broadcast Audience Research Council (BARC) India.

However, cable distribution platforms opposed the move, arguing that the exclusion could adversely affect distribution dynamics and the commercial arrangements between broadcasters and cable operators.

Path cleared for implementation

With the interim stay now vacated and AIDCF withdrawing its petition, industry participants believe the government has effectively removed the biggest legal obstacle to implementing the revised ratings framework.

While AIDCF has retained the right to file a fresh petition in the future, executives said the operators will not pursue the matter.

"The withdrawal suggests they recognised that pursuing the existing petition was unlikely to produce the outcome they wanted. If they return, it will probably have to be with a different legal strategy and stronger constitutional arguments," another senior executive said.

The latest development is expected to accelerate the rollout of television ratings that exclude landing page viewership, a reform that broadcasters have argued will improve the credibility and integrity of audience measurement by ensuring ratings better reflect deliberate viewer consumption rather than automatic channel exposure.

Industry executives also expect the move to enable BARC India to operationalize the revised measurement framework without the legal uncertainty that had persisted since the policy was notified earlier this year. They said the focus is now likely to shift toward implementation timelines, methodological refinements and the industry's longer-term roadmap for expanding television measurement, including connected TV (CTV) audiences.
