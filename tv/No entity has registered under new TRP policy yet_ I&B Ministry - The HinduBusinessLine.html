No entity has registered under new TRP policy yet: I&B Ministry

Union Minister L Murugan says new policy defines clear standards for registration, operation, audit, and oversight of agencies providing TV rating services

By BL New Delhi Bureau

Updated - July 24, 2026 at 09:12 PM.

The Ministry of Information & Broadcasting (MIB) on Friday informed the Parliament that no entity is registered under the newly released Television Ratings Policy, 2026. This comes at a time when release of TRP ratings by the Broadcast Audience Research Council remains suspended pending compliance with the new regulatory framework.

Advertisement

Union Minister of State for Information and Broadcasting and Parliamentary Affairs L Murugan, in a written reply in Rajya Sabha said, “As on date, no entity is registered under the new Policy. The Television Ratings Policy, 2026 places no restriction on the number of agencies that may be registered to provide TV rating services.”

He said that the Ministry’s new policy defines clear standards for the registration, operation, audit, and oversight of agencies providing TV rating services, with the aim of ensuring transparency, independence and accountability in audience measurement. The TV Rating Policy 2026 replaced the Guidelines for TV Rating Agencies in India.

The policy reduced the net worth criteria from ₹20 crore to ₹5 crore and has increased sample size by expansion of metered homes from 50,000 to 80,000. It also excluded landing page in the computation of ratings while focusing on technology-neutral audience measurement across multiple platforms including connected TVs and TV channels available on OTT Platforms among other provisions.

“Under this Policy, any entity in the business of television ratings in India has to comply with certain additional requirements and to register under the new Policy. The prescribed safeguards include cross-holding restrictions, requirement of at least 33 per cent independent directors on the board of the agency, prohibition of consultancy or advisory activities that may create conflicts of interest, security clearance requirements, audit and transparency obligations to ensure the functional and operational independence of rating agencies,” he stated in his response.
